# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two overlapping audiences: (1) recruiters and hiring managers evaluating candidacy for AI/data/program-management roles, who need fast, credible evidence of impact; (2) a broader professional network — academic contacts, research collaborators, hackathon and conference connections — who encounter the site as a professional front door beyond job applications specifically. Both arrive to assess credibility and decide whether reaching out is worth their time.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that makes the case for Yashaswi as a hire or collaborator through editorial-quality storytelling: real project evidence (case studies, research, leadership) instead of generic template content. Success is a visitor reaching out directly (email, LinkedIn message, or scheduling a call), not just passive browsing.

## Brand Personality

Editorial, warm, credible. The voice reads like a considered magazine feature about the person's work, not a SaaS landing page or a cold engineering resume. Confidence without hype: claims are backed by specific, measurable outcomes, never bare adjectives.

## Anti-references

No specific named sites to avoid, but implicitly: generic AI-slop SaaS templates (gradient hero, eyebrow kickers above every section, identical card grids, the "big number, small label" hero-metric cliche), and cold minimalist tech-portfolio patterns that strip out warmth and personality. The existing editorial direction is trusted; refine it rather than introducing a new aesthetic lane.

## Design Principles

- Evidence over adjectives: every claim is backed by a specific, measurable outcome.
- Editorial voice, not marketing voice: reads like a magazine feature, not a pitch deck.
- One singular accent color (oat) carries the "look here" work; restraint everywhere else.
- Warmth without softness: welcoming tone, but every section still earns credibility through rigor, real metrics, real photos, real case studies.
- Direct path to contact: the site's job is to make reaching out feel like the natural next step.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA (4.5:1 body text contrast, 3:1 large text) is the enforced floor, already implemented via the ink-muted/accent2 token adjustments. Reduced motion is honored three ways: CSS media query, framer `MotionConfig`, and manual guards in canvas components. No stricter requirement right now — maintain this baseline, don't regress it.
